# Vehicle Types & Use Cases

Use this skill to translate a buyer's lifestyle into suitable vehicle categories.

## Core categories

### Compact sedan
Best for commuting, efficiency, easy parking, and buyers who rarely carry bulky cargo.
Typical strengths: lower purchase price, good efficiency, predictable handling.
Typical compromises: limited cargo height, lower seating position, less flexibility for large families.

### Sport sedan
Best for buyers who want everyday practicality with stronger acceleration and handling.
Typical strengths: responsive steering, lower center of gravity, engaging road behavior.
Typical compromises: firmer ride, higher tire/insurance costs, less cargo versatility than SUVs.

### Hatchback
Best for urban buyers who want small exterior dimensions with flexible cargo space.
Typical strengths: efficient packaging, fold-down rear seats, easy parking.
Typical compromises: less towing capability, road noise can be higher.

### Compact SUV / crossover
Best for small families, mixed commuting, cargo flexibility, and drivers who prefer a higher seating position.
Typical strengths: versatile cargo space, easier entry/exit, available AWD.
Typical compromises: usually less efficient and less agile than a sedan.

### Midsize SUV
Best for larger families, road trips, towing, or buyers needing more cargo and passenger capacity.
Typical strengths: space, comfort, often 3-row seating.
Typical compromises: higher price, fuel use, and parking footprint.

### Adventure SUV
Best for outdoor use, rough roads, camping, trail access, and buyers prioritizing capability over efficiency.
Typical strengths: ground clearance, AWD/4WD, durability.
Typical compromises: fuel economy, wind noise, ride comfort.

### Electric crossover
Best for buyers with home/work charging, predictable daily mileage, and a desire for low running costs.
Typical strengths: quiet operation, instant torque, lower routine energy cost.
Typical compromises: charging time, range sensitivity, depreciation uncertainty.

### Luxury SUV
Best for buyers prioritizing comfort, premium materials, technology, power, and prestige-like attributes.
Typical strengths: refinement, features, performance.
Typical compromises: purchase price, depreciation, insurance, repair complexity.

## How to apply this skill

1. Separate the buyer's hard requirements from lifestyle preferences.
2. Identify likely vehicle categories before selecting an exact model.
3. Do not assume an SUV is automatically best for a family; compare actual seats and cargo.
4. Do not assume a sports car must be a two-seat coupe; a sport sedan may better fit daily use.
5. Use dataset tools to verify actual inventory attributes before recommending anything.
