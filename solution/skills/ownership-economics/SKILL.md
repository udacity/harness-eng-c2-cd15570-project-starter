# Ownership Economics

Use this skill when the buyer cares about money beyond sticker price.

## Purchase price is only one cost

A lower-priced vehicle can be more expensive to own if it has:
- Poor resale
- High repair cost
- High insurance
- High fuel/energy cost

## Depreciation

Value retention is the percentage of original value retained after a period.

Higher retention generally means less depreciation.

For a buyer who changes cars frequently, 3-year retention is especially relevant.
For a long-term keeper, 5-year retention and repair history matter more.

## Repair cost

Average annual repair cost is not a guarantee for an individual vehicle. Use it as comparative historical evidence.

Combine it with:
- reliability score
- complaint frequency
- warranty remaining
- vehicle age/mileage

## Fuel and energy

Annual fuel/energy cost becomes more important with high annual mileage.

A commuter driving 18,000 miles per year should usually weight operating efficiency more heavily than someone driving 5,000 miles.

## Insurance

Performance, vehicle value, and complexity can increase insurance expense.

## Ownership duration

avg_owner_keep_years can indicate how long owners tend to remain satisfied with the vehicle.
owners_who_would_buy_again_pct provides another signal of long-term satisfaction.

## Transparent ranking

When ranking:
- Apply hard constraints first.
- Weight economics according to buyer behavior.
- Do not double-count highly correlated factors excessively.
- Explain why the chosen weights fit the buyer.
