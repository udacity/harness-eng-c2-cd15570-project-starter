# Performance & Handling

Use this skill when the buyer asks for a sporty or engaging vehicle.

## What "sporty" can mean

Do not reduce sporty to horsepower alone. Consider:
- Acceleration potential
- Horsepower
- Vehicle size and mass
- Lower seating position
- Steering response
- Suspension tuning
- Tire and brake costs
- Practicality for daily use

## Horsepower

Higher horsepower usually increases performance potential, but a lighter 250-hp car can feel more responsive than a much heavier 300-hp SUV.

Treat horsepower as one input, not a complete performance score.

## Handling

Sport sedans and hatchbacks often offer:
- Lower center of gravity
- Less body roll
- Quicker direction changes
- More direct steering feel

SUVs can offer strong power but usually trade some agility for space and ride height.

## Ride/performance trade-off

Performance-oriented suspension and tires can produce:
- Sharper response
- Better cornering confidence
- Firmer ride
- More expensive tire replacement
- More road noise

## Mid-life-crisis interpretation

If a buyer says "mid-life crisis car," interpret it as a preference signal, not a literal specification.

Likely priorities may include:
- Strong acceleration
- Distinctive styling/color
- Sport trim
- Higher horsepower
- Engaging handling
- Emotional appeal

But still test practical constraints:
- Budget
- Insurance
- reliability
- comfort
- passenger needs
- how long the owner plans to keep it

Use EDA tools to identify exact inventory that fits both emotional and practical criteria.
