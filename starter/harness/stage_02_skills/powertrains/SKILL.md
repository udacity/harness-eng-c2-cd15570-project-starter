# Powertrains & Engine Technology

Use this skill when the buyer's driving pattern makes propulsion technology important.

## Gasoline internal-combustion engine

Energy comes from fuel burned in cylinders. Power depends on displacement, aspiration, tuning, gearing, and vehicle mass.

Strengths:
- Fast refueling
- Broad infrastructure
- Familiar service ecosystem
- Often attractive for high-performance or towing applications

Trade-offs:
- Fuel cost exposure
- More routine mechanical maintenance
- Lower efficiency in stop-and-go operation

## Hybrid

Combines an internal-combustion engine with an electric motor and small battery. The system can recover braking energy and use the motor to reduce engine workload.

Best fit:
- High annual mileage
- Mixed city/highway commuting
- Buyers without convenient charging

Strengths:
- Strong fuel economy
- No charging requirement
- Particularly efficient in urban driving

Trade-offs:
- Added drivetrain complexity
- Usually modest electric-only operation

## Plug-in hybrid (PHEV)

Combines an engine with a larger battery that can be externally charged.

Best fit:
- Daily commute fits within electric range
- Buyer can charge regularly
- Buyer still wants gasoline for long trips

Strengths:
- Electric commuting plus long-trip flexibility
- Can dramatically reduce fuel use when charged consistently

Trade-offs:
- More components and complexity
- Benefits decline if the owner rarely plugs in
- Often higher purchase price

## Battery electric vehicle (EV)

Uses electric motors powered by a traction battery.

Best fit:
- Home/work charging available
- Predictable daily driving
- Buyer values low energy cost and smooth acceleration

Strengths:
- Instant torque
- Low routine energy cost
- Fewer conventional drivetrain maintenance items
- Quiet operation

Trade-offs:
- Charging time
- Range varies with speed, weather, and climate use
- Long-distance convenience depends on charging access

## Useful technical concepts

### Horsepower
A measure of power output. Higher horsepower can support stronger acceleration and higher sustained performance, but vehicle mass and gearing matter.

### Torque
Rotational force. Electric motors can deliver high torque immediately, which is why EVs often feel quick at low speed.

### MPG
Miles traveled per gallon. Higher is generally better for gasoline/hybrid efficiency.

### EV range
Estimated distance available on battery energy. Treat stated range as a planning value, not a guaranteed real-world distance.

## Decision guidance

Match propulsion to use pattern, not ideology:
- Heavy commuter without charging -> hybrid may be compelling.
- Predictable commute plus home charging -> EV may minimize running cost.
- Short daily commute plus frequent long trips -> PHEV can fit well.
- Performance/towing with limited charging access -> gasoline may remain practical.

Always use the dataset for the actual MPG, range, price, reliability, and ownership-cost evidence.
